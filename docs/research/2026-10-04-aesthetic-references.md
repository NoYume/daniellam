# Aesthetic references for the experimental directions (2026-10-04)

**Why this exists.** On 2026-10-04 Daniel asked for reference websites that match four aesthetics:
- Dreampunk
- Cyberprep
- Sinofuturism
- Neo-Tokyo

His feedback on the v2 experiment:
- "The font choice is too unprofessional for a website that recruiters will be seeing and researchers."
- "The overall design seems extremely generic."

That experiment used Michroma, dark navy, stars and generated mountains. He did like the vertical Chinese characters next to each section title.

**How it was gathered.** Two research agents covered two aesthetics each. A third agent found stand-in photos.
- Sites were checked with WebFetch and read-only curl. Fonts and colors come from each site's own CSS.
- Both research agents hit the session's 200-search cap, so discovery was cut short. That is why few personal portfolios appear.
- The agents could not see screenshots. In Chrome, most of these sites load behind a loading screen or a cookie banner, so visual claims about WebGL-heavy sites are inferred from their code.

## Definitions

**Sinofuturism**
- The term comes from Lawrence Lek's 2016 video essay "Sinofuturism (1839-2046 AD)" ([lawrencelek.com](https://lawrencelek.com/works/sinofuturism)). It reads China's technology as a kind of AI.
- Visual vocabulary: infrastructure, smart cities, surveillance, space, and Chongqing, Shanghai and Shenzhen. Key colors: "red, blue, beige, metallics" ([Aesthetics Wiki](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Sinofuturism)). Chinese characters are used as visual form.

**Neo-Tokyo**
- Comes from Akira: megastructures, neon, kanji and katakana signs, rain, night ([Aesthetics Wiki](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Neo-Tokyo)).
- Ghost in the Shell (1995) modeled its city on Hong Kong's signs, which bridges it to Sinofuturism.
- The Designers Republic's Wipeout (1995) graphics set the graphic grammar: Japanese type, fake corporate marks, flat vectors.

**Dreampunk**
- Mainly a music microgenre: cinematic ambient that split off from vaporwave in the mid-2010s, defined on the Dream Catalogue label. It evokes "navigating a vast, futuristic metropolis, often at night and in the rain."
- Its look comes from album covers: rainy neon cities, lone figures, deep blues and black. Influences include Wong Kar-wai and Blade Runner.
- The visual definition is thin. ([Aesthetics Wiki](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Dreampunk), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreampunk))

**Cyberprep**
- "High tech, high life": cyberpunk-level technology in a utopia, in white and light blue. It overlaps Y2K Futurism and Frutiger Aero.
- The definition is contested. Aesthetics Wiki flags its page "Needs work", and Wikipedia treats it as close to postcyberpunk. ([Aesthetics Wiki](https://aesthetics.fandom.com/wiki/Cyberprep))

**Related looks**
- **Gen X Soft Club** (CARI): a muted take on Y2K. Helvetica in the style of subway signs; metros, airports and skylines; blur, bloom, bokeh; blues, beiges and greys. Its media list includes Wong Kar-wai's *Chungking Express* and *Fallen Angels*. This is the closest bridge between Dreampunk and a professional site. ([cari.institute](https://cari.institute/aesthetics/gen-x-soft-club))
- **Frutiger Aero:** glossy skies, water and bubbles, in blue, green and white.
- **City Pop / Hiroshi Nagai:** empty paradises under ultramarine skies.

## Non-website references

- **Hong Kong MTR signage**
  - Station names are in calligraphy, chosen for its "sense of motion" ([MTR](https://www.mtr.com.hk/en/corporate/publications/mtr-calligraphy.html)).
  - The system pairs the MTR's own Song typeface with Myriad (secondary source).
  - Prototype v3 B echoes this with Chiron Sung HK and Source Sans 3.
- **Tokyo Metro numbering (2004):** a line letter plus a number in a colored circle, such as G-09. It inspired the "D" line bullet in v3 B.
- **Hong Kong neon:** hand-bent tubes with Chinese lettering, collected by [M+](https://www.mplus.org.hk/en/magazine/collecting-conserving-exhibiting-neon-signs-mplus-museum-hong-kong/).
- **Xu Bing's Square Word Calligraphy:** Latin letters built into the shape of Chinese characters. A possible "DL" monogram later.
- **Blue-and-white porcelain:** cobalt on white, which maps onto his denim and white taste.

## Type lessons

- Professional bilingual sites set Chinese in Source Han Sans, Noto Sans CJK or HarmonyOS Sans. Wide techno fonts (Novecento Wide, Geometos, Purista) appear only as tiny caps labels. Michroma failed because it was used as the main typeface.
- **The safest Latin + Chinese pair is Source Sans 3 with Source Han Sans (Noto Sans TC).** Source Han Sans takes its Latin letters from Source Sans. Prototype v3 B uses this pair.
- **Other free pairings suggested:**
  - **Signage:** Switzer, Fragment Mono (a monospaced Helvetica) and Noto Sans TC.
  - **Robotic Cyberprep:** Hubot Sans at regular width only, Mona Sans, JetBrains Mono and Noto Sans TC.
  - **Film title:** Zodiak or Newsreader, Switzer, Noto Serif TC and JetBrains Mono. This is A and C's current system.
- **Chinese faces confirmed on Google Fonts (2026-10-04):**
  - Chiron Sung HK and Chiron Hei HK (Hong Kong Song and Hei)
  - LXGW WenKai TC (Kai, handwriting style)
  - Noto Sans TC and Noto Serif TC
  - Doto (dot-matrix, for the one LED detail) is also available.
